* Background

I started to join AtCoder contests 15 months ago. I've been using Haskell, because I wanted to know how a "purely functional" programming language works.

And it worked. I've reached a rating of 1,200, meaning that I'm in the top 8% of players, and one of the "light blue" coders by rating color. I'm glad to be one of them!

In this article, I will share my competitive programming experience and thoughts on writing Haskell.

** What is AtCoder?

[[https://atcoder.jp/][AtCoder]] is a website that hosts competitive programming contests. If you know [[https://codeforces.com/][Codeforces]], AtCoder is an alternative to it.

AtCoder is also a popular host. Around 10,000 people join its weekly contests, and it holds the AtCoder World Tour Finals for the top players in the world.

** What is competitive programming?

Competitive programming is basically about algorithms, data structures and math. You're given some problems to solve in a limited time (e.g., 100 minutes). The problem input is given as stdin, and you use stdout for the answer.

Most problems reject naive answers as too slow. $O(n^2)$ algorithms will be rejected on large inputs such as $n = 1,000,000$. You will try to reduce the time complexity to $O(n \log n)$, or even faster. That's what we're addicted to!

* The journey

** Algorithms and data structures

The first time I joined a contest, I solved only two or three problems. My solutions exceeded the time limit, and I had no idea why. "I'm dumb! No one loves me..."

Then I read algorithm books and editorials, and slowly got the idea. Cumulative sums, Union-Find tree (a.k.a. disjoint set union), Fermat's little theorem, dynamic programming, graph search methods... I had to collect all that knowledge and those methods.

After solving hundreds of problems, the moment came. "I'm a genius! Everyone loves me...."

** Haskell

In my case, it was even harder, because I was using Haskell for the first time. It didn't even compile and gave me such complex messages. It was often due to type mismatch, indentation error, missing argument or operator precedence error, but none of them were indicated directly. I had to figure out the root cause for every error message, and I made little progress.

After six months, I was able to write basic Haskell, but the rating was very poor. It was less than 400, which classified me as a "beginner". I had years of programming experience in C++, C#, Rust and Python, but none of it helped. Haskell beat me. I was not who I hoped I was.

I was beaten every day by every problem. Someday, I may be someone I wanted to be, but it was not today, tomorrow or the next month. I couldn't identify with that person. I was someone who just suffered from Haskell and competitive programming. I had to have some courage to accept that truth.

Today I'm probably that person, but it does not rewrite the history.

** Purely functional programming

What did I get after the long journey? I learned a lot of algorithms and data structures, that's cool. What about Haskell? It's known for purity, lazy evaluation, and monads. Did it change the way I program and think?

Yes! But in a minor way. Purity, laziness and monads mattered less than I expected. The biggest difference was in handling temporary states.

In Rust, for instance, we often have a temporarily mutable variable and work on it:

#+BEGIN_SRC rust
let mut map = HashMap::new();
let mut result = 1usize;
for x in &xs {
    if let Some(&count) = map.get(x) {
        map.insert(x, count + 1);
        result *= count;
    } else {
        map.insert(x, 1);
    }
}
#+END_SRC

In Haskell, we would use a folding function for this kind of computation:

#+BEGIN_SRC haskell
let (!_, !result) = foldl' f (HM.empty, 1 :: Int) xs
      where
        f (!m, !res) x = case HM.lookup x m of
          Just count -> (HM.insert x (count + 1) m, res * count)
          Nothing -> (HM.insert x 1 m, res)
#+END_SRC

This one is still basic, but even in more complex situations, we stick with the idea of "states as new values". It may look very odd, but now I like this very explicit style.

* Wrapping up

I kept learning Haskell and competitive programming over the past 15 months, and finally reached a rating of 1,200! I experienced some despair, courage, and purely functional programming. Let's keep making these kinds of changes.
